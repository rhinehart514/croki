Motion audit · running workflow · design-motion-principles

The board is alive. It doesn't yet perform.

Drover already has a real motion system — one spring drives every reveal and list, and a run genuinely animates: nodes glow, edges flow, the crew springs in. The gap is that all of it fires at once and independently, so nothing tells your eye where the work is right now, and what just changed. This is what a single, staged performance looks like instead — held to a work-tool bar, product-wide.

Overall

The foundation is better than most shipped products — nothing hard-cuts, everything animates out as well as in, and a run has real feedback. It's not under-animated. It's over-simultaneous.

During a live run, six-plus continuous loops breathe at the same time: the active-node glow, a think-pulse, the marching edge dashes, the lane shimmer, the "live" orb, and the crew bob. When everything pulses, pulsing stops meaning anything — the eye can't find the one node where work actually is. And those run-time loops don't honor reduced-motion, so a vestibular-sensitive founder gets all of it, forever. The fix isn't more motion or less motion. It's choreography: reserve continuous movement for the single active step, and turn every hand-off into one legible through-line — done check, edge draws to the next node, next node lights, crew glides across. One performance the eye can follow from goal to gate.

Where the timings land

  • 1 Crew springs in over node 240ms
  • 2 Edge draws to next node 460ms
  • 3 Step-done flash 700ms
  • 4 Glow · pulse · shimmer · dash · orb · bob ∞ loop

What's off

The enters are right — the crew springing in at 240ms is dead-on for a work tool. The problem lives at the two ends of the scale.

The step-done flash is slow (700ms) and then gone — a completion should register fast and stay registered, not linger then vanish.

Everything at "∞" is the real issue. Half a dozen animations never stop. In a work tool, continuous motion should be rationed to the one thing happening now; here it's the wallpaper.

Issues to address
CriticalRestraintAccessibility

The run's continuous loops ignore reduced-motion

WhatThe animations that run during a workflow — the node glow, think-pulse, edge march, lane shimmer, live orb, replay pulse — all loop infinite with no prefers-reduced-motion escape. A founder who's set their system to reduce motion still gets every loop, indefinitely.

Why it mattersThis is the one hard gate in motion work — continuous, un-pausable movement is a vestibular trigger, and a run is the exact moment the screen is busiest. It's also the fix that costs the least.

Recommended motionUnder reduced-motion, collapse every run-time loop to a static state signal — the active node simply reads "active" with a still ring and a tinted icon, no breathing. Motion off; meaning intact.

CriticalRestraint

Everything breathes at once, so nothing points anywhere

WhatAt any moment in a run, six-plus loops animate simultaneously across the canvas, rail, and composer. The active node glows, but so does the live orb, the lane shimmer, the marching edges, and the crew bob — all at similar volume.

Why it mattersMovement is the strongest thing the eye tracks. If it's everywhere, it's the same as nowhere — the founder can't glance at the board and instantly know which step is working. The most valuable signal in the product is being spent on wallpaper.

Recommended motionOne focus at a time. Only the single node doing work moves; every other node holds perfectly still and dims slightly. As work advances, the "live" state travels from node to node — so movement itself becomes the answer to "where are we right now."

Issues to address
ImportantPolishContinuity

Steps hand off with no through-line

WhatWhen one step finishes and the next begins, the two events fire independently — a flash here, a glow there. Nothing visually connects them, so the eye has to re-find where the work moved.

Why it mattersThe single most important thing a running board can tell you is the work just moved from here to there. Right now that's left to chance. A hand-off is the natural place to spend a beat of motion because it carries real information.

Recommended motionChoreograph the baton-pass as one sequence: the finished node's check draws on, the edge draws toward the next node, the next node lights as the edge lands. ~600ms end to end, and the eye rides it.

ImportantPolish

"Done" is a flash, not a state that stays

WhatA completed step flashes for 700ms and then looks like every idle node again. There's no durable, scannable mark that says "this one's finished."

Why it mattersA founder scanning a run needs to read progress at a glance — what's done, what's live, what's ahead. If done-ness evaporates, they can't. The shipped pattern is steps that check off and stay checked, the same receipts logic you already built for the history trail.

Recommended motionReplace the flash with a check that draws on over ~320ms and holds. The node keeps a quiet "done" ring afterward — progress becomes legible without any motion at all once it's settled.

ImportantPolishRestraint

The gate — the whole point of a run — arrives at the same volume as everything else

WhatThe founder gate blooming its decidable card is the climax of every run: it's the moment you're asked to decide. Today it appears amid all the ambient pulsing, so it doesn't land as the event it is.

Why it mattersBy the frequency rule, a rare high-stakes moment is exactly where expressive motion is earned — the opposite of the constant loops. But it only reads as special if the ambience around it goes quiet first. Restraint everywhere else is what buys the gate its moment.

Recommended motionOnce the loops are rationed (findings 1–2), let the gate be the one expressive spring: the card blooms up from its node — a real origin, not fading in from center — settling in ~380ms. Amber stays reserved for exactly this.

Lens reference

Judged primarily by restraint, secondarily by production polish.

Why: Drover is a work tool a founder lives in daily, so the bar is "most motion shouldn't exist, and what does must earn its place by how rarely you meet it." The system already has the polish plumbing; what it needs is the discipline to ration continuous motion and choreograph the few moments that carry information.

To lean harder on restraint: cut every ambient loop that isn't the active step, and make completion a static state rather than an animation once it's settled.

To lean harder on polish: invest the baton-pass and the gate bloom with shared-element continuity (the crew and the edge as one moving object), and mirror every enter with a subtler exit.
