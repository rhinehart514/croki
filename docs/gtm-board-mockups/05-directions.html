What it's actually for

Five ways to say the value. Tell me which one feels true.

Same product, different promise. The one that makes you nod is the one we lead with everywhere — the headline, the home screen, the pitch.

01 — the doer

"Describe the goal. It runs your go-to-market."

You're the founder, not a marketer. Say what you want in a sentence; it finds the people, writes the outreach, runs it — you just approve.

Best if the pain is "I don't have time to do GTM"
02 — the map

"See your whole go-to-market in one place."

Where you stand on everything that matters — who you sell to, what's working, what's still a guess — and the one thing worth doing next.

Best if the pain is "I have no idea where I stand"
03 — the lab

"Run your sales like experiments."

Try a way of reaching people, see if it works, keep it or kill it. Every guess becomes a test, and the winners become what you know.

Best if the pain is "I'm guessing and nothing's repeatable"
04 — the truth

"It reads your product, so it never oversells."

It looks at what your product actually does before it writes a word. No made-up claims, no promises you can't keep — only what's real.

Best if the pain is "AI tools write generic lies"
05 — the compounding

"Every approval teaches it your taste."

Each time you say yes, no, or "change this," it learns. The tenth message sounds like you wrote it. The work gets better while you sleep.

Best if the pain is "AI never sounds like me"

The whole journey, plainly

Getting customers is eight questions. The product answers them with you.

This is the entire job, start to finish — not a feature list. Whatever shape we build, it has to make this loop feel obvious and doable. Each box is a question you must answer; the small line is what the product does there.

Who's it for
Who actually buys this?
Suggests who to target from your product, lets you try a few and compare.
Why now
What makes them ready today?
Finds the moments that make someone need you this week.
How you say it
What's the one line?
Drafts a few ways to say it; you A/B the ones that land.
What they buy
The offer and the price?
Helps shape the offer; checks you can actually deliver it.
How you reach them
Where do you find them?
Builds the outreach and runs it — staged for your okay.
Who's talking
Who replied, what stage?
Keeps the real people in one place as they move toward a yes.
What's working
Is any of it landing?
Shows what actually drove replies — and where you're flying blind.
What you learned
Keep, kill, or double down?
Records every call so the next round starts sharper than the last.
↻ …and what you learned (8) feeds back into who's it for (1). It's a loop, not a checklist — that's the whole point.

If you're asking my read

It's not one shape — it's The Guide on the surface, The Map underneath, with a Queue for the boring part.

The Map (what we have) is the truth of the product — but on its own it drops you in an open space with no "now what," which is exactly why it felt confusing. So: open on The Guide — one plain-English "here's where you stand, here's the next move." When you want to explore or go off-script, the whole Map is one zoom out — roam anywhere, summon anything. And the repetitive approving lives in a Queue you clear in minutes. One product, three gears: told what to do, free to explore, fast to approve.
