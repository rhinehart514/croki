One engine, not three canvases

The split-canvas model copies the shared substrate once per channel. There is one RodentRadar engine; channels are routes through it toward different outcomes. Three ways to draw that — each keeps the composer and the canvas as two views of one state.

What's there now — three machines

“Prospect Researcher” and “Outreach Writer” are duplicated nodes across lanes. RodentRadar grounding is repeated at the head of every lane. The shared engine is copied, not shared. Adding a channel means rebuilding the substrate.

The reframe — one engine, channels off it

Grounding, the agent pool, measurement, the gate policy and the learning loop are shared once. A channel is the only thing that diverges — a path from the shared engine to one outcome. Add a channel = draw one new branch, not a new machine.

The forks we have to call before pixels
  1. Shared or copied? When Prospect Researcher serves two channels, is it one node referenced twice (reuse, the engine view) or two instances? This is the data-model decision everything else hangs on.
  2. What is the unit you zoom between? The engine (all channels at once), the channel (one route), or the node? A product thinker wants to start at outcomes and drill down.
  3. Where does the composer act? On the whole engine (“add a content channel”) or scoped to whatever's selected? Selection on canvas = scope for the composer.
  4. Is the gate a node or a property of the engine? If it's the engine's identity (“nothing crosses without you”), it should be one wall every channel passes through, not a box repeated per lane.

B · Layered engine — the gate is a line

The engine reads top to bottom as shared bands. Only the Channels band diverges; everything above and below it is the one engine. The founder gate is a single wall drawn across the whole canvas — every channel visibly crosses it, nothing reaches Stage without you. The composer on the right is editing the same state: a plain-language request renders as a ghosted new channel with inline Accept/Discard — the seam that was broken.
GTM-engineer read: reuse and the wall are both structural, not per-lane. Product-thinker read: it's a system diagram — ground, capability, route, gate, measure, learn. Customer read: “I can see my whole motion and exactly where my approval sits.” Tradeoff: with 6+ channels the channel band needs horizontal scroll or grouping.

A · Engine core, channels as branches

A literal engine: the core holds grounding, buyer, offer and the shared agent pool; channels radiate out, each ending in its own gate→stage→measure but drawing capability from the center. Strength: the “one engine” idea is visceral — power flows from a core. Tradeoff: hub-and-spoke gets crowded past ~4 channels and the gate is buried per-branch instead of being one wall. Best when you have a small number of bold channels.

C · One dedup'd graph — engineer-true

Keep a single directed graph, but a shared node exists once and fans out to every channel that uses it. Sharing is visible as fan-out edges; channels are just the distinct paths to distinct outcomes. Strength: the truest picture of the real engine and its reuse. Tradeoff: closest to a node-graph — needs strong auto-layout or it reads as spaghetti to a non-technical product thinker. The honest engineer's view, riskiest for the customer.
